# Mutation Operators — 12 Operators

Each operator takes one "assumption" as input and returns the broken structure.
Use at least 5 DIFFERENT operators across all ideas. Don't repeat the same operator.
Every result must be explainable: "Which assumption was broken?" + "Which operator was used?"

---

## 1. INVERT (flip the direction)
Swap the positions of two parties considered fixed.
- Buyer ↔ Seller
- The one who pays ↔ The one who is paid
- User ↔ Producer

Example: A restaurant where guests pay to cook their own food.

## 2. DELETE (remove the "mandatory" component)
Remove one element considered essential: customer, product, office, price, sales, login, inventory.

Example: A bank without accounts (a disposable one-time wallet).

## 3. MARKETPLACE-IFY (open it up as a market)
Turn a closed internal asset/process into an open market between parties.

Example: A logistics company opens up its "spare truck capacity" as a public hauling exchange.

## 4. COMPETITOR-AS-CHANNEL (competitor becomes the channel)
Make a competitor your distribution, supplier, or customer.

Example: A small coffee producer supplies grounds to the chain it publicly calls "the enemy".

## 5. CONSTRAINT-AS-PRODUCT (the limitation becomes the product)
Sell the limitation/problem itself as value.

Example: A slow app sold as focus training because it forces users not to multitask.

## 6. UNIT SHIFT (change the pricing unit)
Change what counts as 1 unit of transaction.
- Per month → per healthy day
- Per unit → per failure

Example: Insurance priced per healthy day, with money back when you don't get sick.

## 7. PAYER SWAP (a third party pays)
Shift the payment burden to a third party: sponsor, rival, regulator, the future.

Example: A learning app free for students because companies pay for talent access.

## 8. TIME SHIFT (sell a different time)
Sell the past or future, not now: options, warranties, legacies.

Example: A school pays a scholarship now, claims 5% of salary 10 years later.

## 9. SCALE EXTREME (extreme ×1000 / ÷1000)
Scale up or down until the rules of the game change.

Example: Home laundry ÷1000 → "wash one shirt, delivered by an influencer courier".

## 10. FAILURE-AS-FEATURE (failure becomes the feature)
Turn failure/trash/waste into the selling point.

Example: A game sells a "book of failures" containing every player's defeat.

## 11. OWNERSHIP FLIP (flip ownership)
Users own / the company rents, or vice versa.

Example: Users rent out their phone's screen time to advertisers.

## 12. REMOVE/ADD THE HUMAN (total automation or total manualization)
Take one extreme as the selling point.

Example: "A bank with zero humans" vs "A store run by the owner's hands, 3 days".

---

## Guardrail (required)
- A mutated idea MUST NOT break the law, deceive, or harm innocent parties.
- If a mutation leads there, find a LAWFUL VERSION that still breaks the same assumption.
- Don't stack operators to force "more absurd" — one clean break beats three muddy ones.
