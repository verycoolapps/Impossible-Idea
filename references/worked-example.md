# Worked Example — Two End-to-End Runs (fictional domains)

================================================================
RUN 1 — X = "Coffee shop in Jakarta"
================================================================
📍 CONSENSUS MAP (X = coffee shop)
Assumptions: ① customer=walk-ins ② sells=ready drinks ③ pays=customer
④ competitor=other shops ⑤ distribution=physical ⑥ scarce=free time ⑦ unit=per cup
Untouched: ③ ④ ②

🪜 REJECTION LADDER
CONVENTIONAL -> A "workstation" shop + a monthly subscription.
BORING -> An AI barista that remembers your favorite flavor.
IMPOSSIBLE -> Guests PAY to brew their own coffee. [assumption:③; op:INVERT]
WEIRD -> The shop supplies grounds to a rival chain. [assumption:④; op:COMPETITOR-AS-CHANNEL]
ABSURD -> No menu: guests bring beans, the shop rents space+tools. [assumption:②+③; op:DELETE+OWNERSHIP FLIP]

🧬 CROSSINGS
A. IMPOSSIBLE+WEIRD -> Guests brew their own; the surplus is sold to a rival as "today's guest menu".
B. WEIRD+ABSURD -> A grounds-incubator shop: guests bring beans, the leftover is supplied to a rival.
C. IMPOSSIBLE+ABSURD -> Guests bring beans + pay to brew in a rented space.

👑 GENIUS CANDIDATE: "Guest-Consigned Coffee"
Mechanism: Shop=rented space+incubator; guest=producer&consumer; rival=channel.
Hidden Logic: People pay for "coffee expert" status on socials; the home-roasting community is huge.
Steelman: Home-brewing interest is real; this formalizes it without the shop buying stock.
Pre-mortem: Food-safety rules ban shared kitchens; the rent margin is thin.
Smallest Test: A 1-day pop-up, 10 guests, measure willingness to pay $5 to brew.
Score: Weird 4/5 · Plausible 3/5 · Testable 5/5 · Moat 2/5

================================================================
RUN 2 — X = "EdTech English course"
================================================================
📍 CONSENSUS MAP (X = edtech)
Assumptions: ① customer=learners ② sells=classes ③ pays=learner/parents
④ competitor=platforms ⑤ distribution=app ⑥ scarce=native teachers ⑦ unit=per month
Untouched: ③ ⑥ ⑦

🪜 REJECTION LADDER
CONVENTIONAL -> A live-tutor app per level.
BORING -> An AI tutor chatbot, 24 hours.
IMPOSSIBLE -> Students are paid per day they DON'T use the app (because they're fluent). [assumption:③; op:INVERT]
WEIRD -> Companies pay for talent access; students are free. [assumption:③; op:PAYER SWAP]
ABSURD -> Native teachers rent "listening time" from students practicing speaking. [assumption:⑥+③; op:OWNERSHIP FLIP+INVERT]

🧬 CROSSINGS
A. IMPOSSIBLE+WEIRD -> Students are paid when fluent; companies pay a future premium.
B. WEIRD+ABSURD -> A company sponsors + native teachers rent students' listening time.
C. IMPOSSIBLE+ABSURD -> Students are paid per day they skip; native teachers rent practice time.

👑 GENIUS CANDIDATE: "Reverse Merit Scholarship"
Mechanism: Company pays upfront; students free+bonus if they graduate; app gets talent data.
Hidden Logic: Companies need a cheap talent pipeline; students need external motivation.
Steelman: Income-share is proven in bootcamps; this is its pre-positioning.
Pre-mortem: Hard to measure graduation objectively; companies hesitate to pay for the future.
Smallest Test: 20 students, 1 sponsor, measure retention vs a paid control.
Score: Weird 4/5 · Plausible 4/5 · Testable 4/5 · Moat 3/5
